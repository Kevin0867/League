Four things to settle before anything gets drawn twice

Three of your requirements collide with physics, arithmetic or a utility meter. Here is where they land, and what the rest of this set is designed around.

Constraint · program

Twenty bays do not fit

A bay needs 7'-0" of depth (tub + ramp run + owner) and the aisle needs 6'-0". That consumes the full 20'-0" width, so bays can only run down the two long walls. After a 10'-0" front and a 14'-0" back of house, 26'-0" of wall length is left per side.

Contraindicated · acoustics

Dogs hear the carrier

Parametric "directional sound" domes project a 40–60 kHz ultrasonic carrier at 120 dB+. Dogs hear to roughly 45 kHz. You would be mounting a high-power dog deterrent over every dog. Active noise cancellation is no better — it works below about 500 Hz, and a bark is a broadband 1–4 kHz transient.

45 kHzupper limit, canine
Achievable · acoustics

The hood is the cone of silence

What does work is a per-bay absorptive canopy at 7'-6", solid 6'-0" dividers between bays, a washable NRC 0.90 ceiling and low-level masking. Modelled reverberation drops from about 2.0 s to 0.36 s and an adjacent bark at the owner's ear from ~91 to ~78 dBA.

Go / no-go · utility

Gas decides the lease

Heating 10.6 GPM of hot water through an 85 °F rise is 450,000 BTU/hr. Done with gas that is three wall-hung condensing units. Done with electric resistance it is roughly 132 kW and a 400 A service. Confirm the gas meter before you sign.

Why the module is 7'-0" deep

The tub cabinet is 2'-6" deep. The fold-down ramp runs 4'-0" at a 1:3 slope to clear a 16" rise, and lands 6" clear of the gate. 2'-6" + 4'-0" + 0'-6" = 7'-0". Anything shallower and either the ramp gets steeper than a nervous Labrador will walk, or the owner has nowhere to stand while it is deployed.

You proposed 6'-0" bay widths. That is exactly right for a medium bay and a little tight for a large one, so the set runs three widths: 5'-0" small, 6'-0" medium, 6'-6" large. Two dogs plus one owner fit inside the fence at every size.

The 26'-0" that is left

West wall takes two large plus two medium (25'-0" of bay plus 6" of partitions). East wall takes one large, one medium and two small, plus a 3'-0" towel and supply alcove at the entry end where it catches everyone on the way in.

A maximum-density variant fits five 5'-8" bays per side — ten elevated stations — by shortening the front to 8'-0" and the back to 13'-0". It costs you the large-dog bays, which are the ones people cannot do at home and will pay most for. Not recommended.

Hot water
& pressure

Making ten guns fire at once

"No loss of pressure when everyone is spraying" is two separate problems: enough heat, and enough head. They get two separate answers, and one of them is not a bigger pump.

Heat: 450,000 BTU/hr at full simultaneity

Size on the worst realistic minute, not the average day. Every gun is a 2.0 GPM pressure-compensating nozzle; the design mixed temperature is 100 °F from 55 °F cold and 140 °F stored.

hot fraction = (100 − 55) / (140 − 55) = 45 / 85 = 0.529
per station = 2.0 GPM × 0.529 = 1.06 GPM hot
10 stations = 10.6 GPM hot  (20 GPM mixed)
load = 10.6 GPM × 500 × 85 °F rise = 450,500 BTU/hr
at 60% diversity (realistic peak) → 270,000 BTU/hr
installed = 3 × 199,000 = 597,000 BTU/hr input
output @ 95% = 567,000 = 13.3 GPM at 85 °F rise — 25% margin
any two units alone = 8.9 GPM = 84% of stations. N+1 holds.
  • Three wall-hung 199 MBH condensing tankless on a cascade rack, common-vented, sequenced so they wear evenly.
  • 119-gallon insulated buffer tank downstream. Tankless units hate the 30-second on/off of ten trigger guns; the buffer absorbs it and stops short-cycling.
  • Store at 140 °F for Legionella control, master-mix to 120 °F (ASSE 1017) for distribution, then per-bay mix to 85–105 °F with a hard stop.
  • 3/4" recirculation return with a bronze circulator on an aquastat. Without it the last bay runs 40 seconds of cold water down the drain every single wash — roughly 45,000 gallons a year at 30 washes a day.
  • Twin-alternating softener, 1.5" valve, 25 GPM continuous, if hardness exceeds 7 gpg. Soft water lathers on half the shampoo and keeps scale out of the heat exchangers and the nozzles.

Head: 103 PSI needed at the meter

Add up everything between the street and the worst-case nozzle. This is where most dog washes discover they needed a booster after the walls closed up.

The actual fix for pressure loss A bigger pump alone will not do it — pressure-compensating flow cartridges will. Put a 2.0 GPM compensating cartridge in every spray gun and each one delivers exactly 2.0 GPM whether one bay is running or ten, even if header pressure sags 15 PSI. The VFD booster holds the header steady; the cartridges guarantee every customer gets the same spray. Specify both. Without the cartridges, bay 1 next to the riser steals flow from bay 10 and you will hear about it every Saturday.

Drainage
& dry floor

Drainage, hair, and keeping a dryer out of a puddle

You asked for a surface that drains fast enough that a blow dryer is never near standing water. That is a five-layer answer, and only the last layer is electrical.

Drainage

  • Each tub: 2" waste, deep-seal P-trap, removable 1/8"-perforated stainless strainer basket.
  • Bay floors: slope 1/4" per foot out to the aisle. No bay drains to maintain.
  • Aisle: slopes 1/4" per foot from both sides to a 6" stainless slot channel on the centreline, 32'-0" long, 1/8" per foot fall to a 4" outlet at the back. Hose the entire room down at close.
  • XL bays: 3" × 3'-0" linear drain in each floor pan.
  • Building drain: ~37 DFU total → 4" at 1/8" per foot.
  • Trap primers on every floor drain, electronic manifold in mechanical. A dry trap is a sewer-gas complaint.
The one that ruins operations A 20 GPM hair and solids interceptor is mandatory — flush-mounted in the mechanical room floor, removable stainless basket, emptied daily. Skip it and you will be rodding the building drain monthly within a year. Confirm with the sewer authority whether they also want a sediment interceptor or a discharge permit for shampoo.

Why a dryer never meets water here

  • 1 — The dog stands on a grate. A removable platform 2" above the tub floor. Water leaves under the dog's feet instantly; the airstream hits a dog on a dry-topped surface, not one standing in an inch of runoff.
  • 2 — The tub floor has no flat spots. Factory-pitched 1/4" per foot to a corner drain, coved corners, nothing to pond in.
  • 3 — The bay floor sheds in seconds. Urethane cement with broadcast quartz at 1/4" per foot, DCOF ≥ 0.60 wet. There is no film to stand in and it is not slippery when there is.
  • 4 — The dryer is never on the floor. Wall bracket at 48" AFF, hose on a swivel hook, cord dressed into a strain relief. There is nothing to knock over and nothing to trip on.
  • 5 — Class A GFCI, at the breaker. 4–6 mA trip on a dedicated 20 A circuit per bay, so the whip and the device are both protected — not just a GFCI receptacle. Weather-resistant device, in-use cover, 48" AFF, ≥12" horizontally clear of the rim.

Stated plainly: a corded appliance near water carries residual risk that no design removes. This stack reduces it to very low and makes GFCI the last net rather than the only one. Have the licensed electrical engineer of record confirm the receptacle locations against NEC 210.8(B) and 406.9(C) and your AHJ — those two articles are what the inspector will open the book to.

S-501
Special
Acoustics

The cone of silence, examined honestly

Your instinct is right: a calm dog is a quiet dog, and quiet bays make calm dogs. Your proposed mechanism is the problem. Here is the frequency question you asked, answered, and then the thing to build instead.

Can a dog hear the frequency that gets shot out? Yes.

Parametric arrays — the "audio spotlight" domes sold as directional or private sound — work by modulating audible content onto an ultrasonic carrier, typically 40 to 60 kHz, radiated at 120 dB SPL or more. Dogs hear from roughly 67 Hz to 45 kHz with useful sensitivity well past 30 kHz. A 40 kHz carrier is inside that range. Mounting one over each bay installs a high-power ultrasonic dog deterrent above every dog in the building. It would make them louder, not quieter, and you would never work out why.

Active noise cancellation does not rescue the idea either. ANC is effective below roughly 500 Hz and only within about a tenth of a wavelength of its error microphone — about 2.7 inches at 500 Hz. A bark is a broadband 1–4 kHz transient with a 5–20 ms rise, arriving from a moving source at a moving head. No product creates a silent zone around a person under those conditions. Anything sold as one is selling masking.

What actually delivers the quiet bay

Absorption, barrier, separation, masking, and behaviour — in that order of effect. The canopy over each bay is the piece that does the most work, and it happens to be the shape you were already imagining.

  • Per-bay acoustic canopy. Bay width × 6'-0" × 6" deep, bottom at 7'-6" AFF, 12" downturned skirt on all four sides. Perforated aluminium (23% open) over encapsulated 6 pcf mineral wool, NRC ≥ 0.95. It kills the ceiling bounce — the dominant path from one bay to the next — and puts a metre of absorption directly above the dog's mouth. It is also the exhaust hood, the light housing and the reel mount, so it earns its place four times.
  • Inter-bay dividers at 6'-0", not 4'-0". This is the one change worth making to your brief. A big dog's head sits at about 48" AFF standing in the tub; the owner's ear is at about 64". A 4'-0" divider leaves that path grazing the top — barely 5 dB. Taking the divider between bays to 6'-0" breaks it properly and buys another 6–8 dB, for the price of two feet of HDPE.
  • Keep the aisle fence at 4'-0" exactly as you asked. That is the face staff and other customers look over, and supervision matters more there than acoustics. Solid between bays, 4'-0" picket and gate to the aisle.
  • Washable acoustic ceiling across the wash hall, NRC ≥ 0.90, gasketed aluminium grid, plus washable acoustic wall panels from 4'-0" to 8'-0". Encapsulated cores only — an exposed fibreglass face in a wet dog room is a mould culture.
  • Low-level masking on distributed 70V ceiling speakers: shaped pink noise at 45–48 dBA plus slow, low-frequency classical at 50–60 bpm. Keep total ambient under 55 dBA. Do not fight SPL with SPL — a louder room makes dogs bark harder.
  • Visual separation is the real behavioural lever. Most reactive barking is triggered by seeing another dog, not hearing one. The solid divider removes the dog-height sightline while people still see over the aisle fence. The acoustics and the behaviour want the same wall.

M-601
Mechanical
Air & odor

Capture the smell; don't cover it

Wet dog is volatile microbial metabolites — aldehydes, ketones, sulfur compounds, phenols — released from the coat as water evaporates, and a blow dryer aerosolises the lot. You are right that a scent machine alone will not do it. It needs capture, exhaust, carbon, dilution and dry air, and then a very light hand with fragrance.

Capture and exhaust

  • The canopy is the capture hood. A grille in each canopy face at 8'-0" AFF pulls 175 CFM per elevated bay, 250 CFM per XL bay — right where the dryer is throwing the odour, before it reaches the room.
  • Total exhaust 2,100 CFM (8×175 + 2×250 + restroom 75 + laundry 100), which is 10.5 air changes per hour on a 12,000 CF volume. Grooming spaces want 10–15; a code-minimum ventilation rate would leave this room smelling like a kennel.
  • Make-up air 1,850 CFM tempered through a dedicated ERV, leaving the space 250 CFM net negative. Odour then never migrates to the lobby, the front door, or the neighbouring tenant. In a strip centre this is the difference between a business and a complaint file.
  • Discharge placement: keep the exhaust louvre at least 10'-0" from any outside-air intake, yours or a neighbour's, and downwind of your own entry.

Treatment and drying

  • MERV-13 prefilter → 2" deep-bed activated carbon impregnated with potassium permanganate, on the recirculating side. The permanganate is what takes the aldehydes and amines that plain carbon passes. Change on differential pressure, roughly every six months — put it in the operating budget, because a saturated bed silently stops working.
  • Dehumidify to ≤ 55% RH. Ten dryers evaporating water is a large latent load — on the order of 90 lb/hr, ~97,000 BTU/hr latent. Use DX with hot-gas reheat or a dedicated 130 pt/day commercial unit. Dry air dries dogs faster, holds less odour, and keeps mould out of the canopy insulation.
  • Dose the drains nightly. A bio-enzymatic dosing pump on a timer into the trench and the tub traps. Drain biofilm is the odour source everyone forgets and then blames on the HVAC.
  • No porous materials anywhere. No carpet, no bare wood, no fabric-faced acoustic panels. This is why every absorber in this set is perforated metal over an encapsulated core.
  • Skip bipolar ionisation and PCO unless the unit is UL 2998 zero-ozone listed — the odour evidence is mixed and ozone around animals is not acceptable. Never an ozone generator.
Scent-air machine — read before ordering Fragrance the lobby. Leave the wash hall smelling like nothing. An HVAC-integrated cold-air atomiser in the supply duct, downstream of the carbon, serving Zone A only, dosed light and intermittent. Two reasons. First, a dog's nose is orders of magnitude more sensitive than yours — what reads as pleasant to a customer is overwhelming to the animal you are trying to keep calm. Second, many common diffuser oils are toxic to dogs: tea tree, eucalyptus, pennyroyal, pine, wintergreen, citrus (d-limonene), cinnamon, peppermint and ylang-ylang among them, and cats are more sensitive still. Specify water-based, non-essential-oil fragrance only, and confirm the formulation with the supplier in writing. The wash hall should be odour-neutral by carbon and air change, not masked.

service: 200 A, 120/208 V, 3φ, 4W — headroom to reach 20 bays
  • One dedicated 20 A / 120 V circuit per bay on a Class A GFCI breaker (4–6 mA), so the whip and the device are both protected. Weather-resistant duplex, in-use cover, 48" AFF on the side partition, ≥12" horizontally clear of the tub rim.
  • Class 2 low voltage only below the rim. A 24 VAC transformer per bay feeds the foot switch and the solenoid, with a 6-minute auto-off and a local "water off" button beside the pedal. No line voltage goes anywhere near the wet zone.
  • Lighting: IP65 linear LED integrated into each canopy, 4000 K, CRI ≥ 90, 55 fc at the deck; IP65 downlights at 30 fc in the aisle; 20 fc back of house. High CRI is not a luxury — at low CRI nobody can tell a clean dog from a dirty one, and they will refill the shampoo instead of rinsing.
  • Water heating must be gas. Electric resistance for the same 450 MBH is roughly 132 kW and turns this into a 400 A service with a utility upgrade. If the shell has no gas, price a CO2 heat-pump water heater plant with 500+ gallons of storage before you walk away — it is the only electric route that pencils.
  • Also on the panel: a camera at each bay (liability and remote assist), the leak-detection controller and its motorised shut-off valve, the masking audio head end, the bio-enzymatic drain doser, and a staff master water shut-off at the welcome desk.

Before design development

Confirm early — in this order

  • Gas meter capacity for 597 MBH. The single most common project-killer. Ask the utility before the lease, not after.
  • Electrical service. Does the shell already have 200 A, 120/208 V 3φ, or is that an upgrade you are paying for?
  • Water static and residual pressure measured at 25 GPM, so the booster is sized on a reading rather than an assumption.
  • Sewer authority on hair and shampoo discharge — some require a permit and a sediment interceptor beyond the hair basket.
  • Exhaust discharge location relative to neighbouring intakes, and whether the landlord permits a roof penetration at all.
  • Occupancy classification with the AHJ — likely Group B or M, but confirm, because it drives the restroom count and egress.
  • Animal establishment licensing. Many jurisdictions license self-serve washes even with no grooming service.
  • ADA: accessible route in, a 34" max transaction counter at the desk, a compliant restroom, and XL2 as the accessible wash station.
